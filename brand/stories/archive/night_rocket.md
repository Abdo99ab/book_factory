## Spread 1
Bedtime. {name} at the window. A small rocket parked in the garden with {name}'s initial on the side.

## Spread 2
The control panel: one button says nothing readable but is clearly 'the ask button' (no letters). The comfort object sits in the co-pilot seat.

## Spread 3
Lift-off over {name}'s own street or town (slot: home type and skyline set: city / village / seaside).

## Spread 4
Planet One: everything is upside down. {name} guesses and it gets worse; then {name} asks why — and the planet's people explain the gravity switch.

## Spread 5
{name} flips the switch. Celebration with the planet's people.

## Spread 6
Planet Two: a robot that won't move. {name} asks what it needs — a friend to hold its hand across a bridge.

## Spread 7
Crossing the bridge together, {name} holding the robot's hand.

## Spread 8
Planet Three: quiet gentle creatures who only answer questions that begin with 'how'.

## Spread 9
{name} works out the right 'how' question. The creatures light up.

## Spread 10
A gentle meteor shower (sparkles, not danger). The rocket asks {name} a question for once: which way home? {name} remembers the stars from the window.

## Spread 11
Flying back; the planets and their people wave.

## Spread 12
Landing in the garden. The rocket shrinks to a toy on the grass.

## Spread 13
Morning: {carer} asks 'How did you sleep?' {name}: 'Can I ask YOU a question?'

## Spread 14
Last line: 'The best captains ask the best questions.' Helmet off or visor open on every spread; face always visible.

## Brand essence

Raised Ready makes the child the hero of a story about a real step they are about to take, so they practise it first in the story and then for real.

**Purpose (internal).** Help the adult who reads to a child raise them ready for a world that is changing fast, starting with the steps in front of them this month.

**Promise (customer-facing).** Four parts, each one we can deliver and prove:

1. **It's really them.** Their face on every page, checked by a person before print.
2. **It's their step.** A story about what is happening in your house now, not a generic adventure.
3. **It's your script.** The words to use in the real moment, printed in the book.
4. **It's made to be read again.** Repetition is how a story helps, so the book is built for night after night.

**Beliefs.** Small steps are big ones. Children rehearse in stories. As machines do more, the human skills matter more: asking, judging, being kind, finishing things. Parents need words, not lectures.

**What we are not.** Not a quick fix. Not a lecture. Not anti-tech. Not a gift gimmick. Not a toy shop.

**"Ready for what?" — the ladder.** Each level is said in its own place:

| Level | What we say | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The next step | Potty, nursery, own bed, a new home | Ads, product pages |
| The human skills | Asking good questions, telling true from fake, honesty, kindness | About page, ages 5–10 titles |
| The world they're growing into | Why the brand exists | Brand story, press, community editions |

**Market position.** Every GB incumbent sells the gift and the hero moment; none looks like it knows the step your child is facing. Raised Ready takes the calm, trusted-guide space.

| Brand | How it looks | What it sells | What it leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wonderbly | Whimsical storybook art, warm colour | Occasions, literary gift | Not the child's real face; almost no step titles |
| Hooray Heroes | Bright cartoon avatars, permanent sale banners | Craft, social proof | Reads as a toy, not a guide |
| Tell My Tale | Photo-to-cartoon likeness | Keepsake gift for grandparents | No purpose, no parent script |
| Magical Children's Book | Low-price AI catalogue | Price | Trust |

The "world run by AI" line stays out of GB ads: only 1 of 10 launch titles is AI-world and the phrase reads as fear to a parent buying a potty book.

## Audience and buying moments

Two buyers, one reader: parents buying for a step happening now, and gift buyers (often grandparents) buying for a child they don't live with. Copy always speaks to "the adult who reads to this child", never assumes mum.

| Buyer | Moment | What they need to see | Entry title |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parent of a 2–5-year-old | Potty, nursery start, big bed, a move — usually with a date coming | That it's their child's step, the words to use, that the face really is them | The three Next steps titles |
| Parent of a 5–10-year-old | A worry, honesty, a new home, screens and fake pictures | A story, not a lecture; a question to talk about together | Growing skills, Tomorrow's world |
| Grandparent / aunt / uncle | Christmas, birthday, "just because" | A delightful gift that doesn't imply a problem; how the photo gets added | Lost Dinosaur Egg (gift title), any Adventure |

Gift buyers rarely have the photo or the parent's consent, so the gift flow lets the parent add both later. Grandparents are the biggest buyers and are reached cheapest in daytime ad slots.

**UK English only:** personalised, colour, practise (verb), nappies, nursery, reception, key worker, big bed, bedtime, drop-off, grown-up. Never: diapers, preschool, kindergarten, mom.

## Logo

The logo is 06A Balanced: four separated, rounded steps rising on a diagonal to the left of the wordmark "Raised Ready", set on one line in ink navy. The four steps are the four story families, not levels.

**Construction**

- Steps, bottom to top: rust (Next steps) → teal (Adventures) → sage (Growing skills) → butter (Tomorrow's world). The order never changes.
- Gap between symbol and wordmark: the "Balanced" gap from Round 5 (a deliberate space, still read as one unit). Use the master file; never re-space by eye.
- The wordmark is artwork, not typed text. Never retype it in a font. Master files v1 (22 Oct) redraw 06A from Noto Serif SemiBold, outlined; geometry with the cap height = 100: steps 51 × 29, corner radius 7, each step 55 across and 33 up from the last, symbol-to-wordmark gap 42.

**Versions**

| Version | Use | Ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full colour | Website header, ads end card, back cover, gift card | White, paper or oat |
| One ink (ink navy) | Small print, single-colour print, inside pages | Any light ground |
| One ink (black) | Emboss, foil, printer's mono jobs | Any light ground |
| Reverse (white steps + wordmark) | Dark plates, ink-navy panels | Ink navy, teal |
| Mini R (steps + R) | Favicon, app icon, social avatar, narrow spines | Light or ink navy |

**Clear space and minimum size** *(master files v1)*

- Clear space on every side = the cap height of the "R" in the wordmark.
- Full lockup: at least 30 mm wide in print, 140 px wide on screen. Smaller than that → mini R.
- Mini R (steps + R): at least 64 px; favicons at 16–48 px use the R alone, because the steps blur. Use the mini R alone on any spine under 8 mm.

**Known weak point.** The butter step on white or oat sits at roughly 1.3–1.6 : 1, below the 3 : 1 needed to read as a shape. Logos are exempt from the WCAG contrast rule, and any butter dark enough to reach 3 : 1 stops being yellow, so the fix is a usage rule: full colour at 60 mm / 300 px wide or more, the one-ink version below that. The reverse is always all white: teal and rust nearly vanish on ink navy.

**Never**

- Number the steps, show them as stages, unlocks or ages, or put a family on a "lower" step in copy.
- Recolour, reorder, rotate (except running down a spine), stretch, outline or add effects.
- Add stars, rainbows or other decorations to the mark.
- Lock a tagline into the logo before the ad test picks one.
- Place the full-colour lockup straight onto busy art: use a paper plate or the one-ink version.

## Typography

Locked 22 Oct: Noto Serif SemiBold for titles, Nunito for story, web and email text, DM Mono Medium for bands and labels. All are free Google Fonts, licensed for print and web.

**Fixed roles**

- **Wordmark:** artwork from the 06A master file only.
- **Titles and headlines:** one serif across both routes, so ten titles read as one shelf.
- **Body (story text, web, email):** one rounded or humanist sans.
- **Labels and bands:** one monospace or small-caps style, in capitals with wide tracking.

**Options considered (T-A chosen)**

| Option | Titles | Body | Labels | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-A | Noto Serif SemiBold (the wordmark's source) | Nunito | DM Mono | Titles and logo match; warmth comes from Nunito and the art |

## Illustration

Two illustration languages, one rule above both: the child from the photo is recognisable on every spread, in a real place, doing something a child can copy.

|  | Morning Light (ages 2–5) | Field Guide (ages 5–10) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | Matte gouache, visible brush and paper texture | Fine charcoal ink line + translucent watercolour wash |
| Light and colour | Warm daylight; oat, rust, sage, butter | Paper white; teal, sage, butter accents |
| Settings | Real UK homes: terraces, nursery coat pegs, wellies, rain on windows | Real UK places, observed: classroom, kitchen table, park, library |
| Viewpoint | Child's eye level; an adult's hand or legs often in frame | Slightly wider, observational; room for noticing details |
| Details | Soft, few, large shapes | Small maps, labels, arrows, "spot it" details — details, never worksheets |
| Proportions | Toddler / preschool | Older, less rounded faces and bodies |
| Avoid | Glossy 3D, plastic skin, toy-shop saturation | Anime styling, glossy colour, cluttered pages |

**Shared rules (from the book-design evidence)**

1. The child is human, realistic and central on every spread; other characters appear whom the child helps or understands.
2. Real settings and a real-world solution the child can copy. No magic solves the problem.
3. Show the wanted behaviour and its reward; never show punishment or the unwanted behaviour in detail.
4. One target step or behaviour per book.
5. Never introduce the fear the book is about (no monsters in a bedtime or worry book).
6. Plain pages: no clutter, no flaps; leave a calm zone for the text on every spread.
7. Every title has one fixed **anchor object** (e.g. the rabbit, Pip the dinosaur, the detective notebook) drawn the same way on every spread: it is the reference for the face and style check.
8. No text, letters or logos inside the art. All words are set in code.
9. Screens, if shown, show nothing readable; no real AI tool is shown being used by a child.

**Resolution for print** (working format 210 × 210 mm + 4 mm bleed)

| Item | Size with bleed | Pixels at 300 ppi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single page | 218 × 218 mm | 2,575 × 2,575 px |
| Double-page spread | 428 × 218 mm | 5,055 × 2,575 px |
| Cover wrap | From the printer's template | At 300 ppi |

Round 8 art is 1,254 px, which prints at about 153 ppi: too soft. If the photo-to-image system outputs under 2,500 px, add an upscaling step before the print file.

**Face check before every print** (the human check in the promise): face shape, skin tone, eye colour, hair colour and texture, glasses or marks match the photo on every spread; the anchor object matches; hands, fingers and eyes have no artefacts. One failed spread means that spread is redrawn before the preview goes to the parent.

## Book format and page structure

Every title uses the same 32-page frame: a title page, 14 story spreads and a closing parent spread, so the production pipeline never changes between titles.

**Working format** (locked 22 Oct; Book Factory is being set to it; the printer confirms its template): 210 × 210 mm trim, 32 interior pages, 170 gsm uncoated, case-bound hardcover with laminated printed cover, PUR binding. Bleed 4 mm on every edge.

**Safe zones:** text and faces at least 10 mm from the trim and at least 15 mm from the gutter (the spine fold eats into a case-bound page). No face ever sits across the gutter.

| Pages | Content | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title page: band, title, "Illustrated from \[Name\]'s photo" | Right-hand page, one small spot illustration |
| 2–29 | 14 story spreads | Text set in code over a calm zone of the art |
| 30–31 | "Words for the real moment" spread (see that section) | Same layout in every title |
| 32 | Dedication ("For \[Name\], from \[giver\]"), read-it-again note, small logo, order code | Last left-hand page |

**Words per spread:** 30–50 for ages 3–6, 50–70 for ages 5–8, 70–90 for ages 7–10.

**To confirm with the printer before files are built:** whether endpapers are printed or plain and whether they count in the 32 pages, the exact cover-wrap and spine template, the PDF page order, and where the batch code sits in the trim area.

## Covers

The front cover sells the child's face at thumbnail size; everything else on it serves that.

**Front cover hierarchy:** 1. the family band, 2. the child's face, 3. the title, 4. the logo.

- **Band:** full width across the top, 13 mm deep below the trim (plus bleed), in the family colour, band text in capitals.
- **Art:** text-free, with a calm zone planned for the title.
- **Title:** "\[Name\] and the" on its own line, the rest on 1–2 lines.
- **Logo:** full lockup bottom centre on a calm area, or one-ink on busy art.

**Layout — full-bleed chosen 22 Oct:**

| Option | How it works | Strength | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boxed (Round 8) | Title on a paper panel, art in a window below | Very consistent shelf | Art is \~45 % of the cover; the face is small in a 300 px ad or shop thumbnail |
| Full-bleed | Art fills the cover; band on top; title over a calm part of the art | The face is the biggest thing on the cover | Each illustration needs a planned title zone |

**Spine** (width from the printer's template): title reading top to bottom, 8 pt, and the mini R at the foot. On spines under 8 mm, mini R only. Spine ground = family colour.

**Back cover:**

- The question form at the top ("Ready for adventure, Ava?").
- A blurb of up to 45 words: what happens in the story, no outcome claim.
- One line: "Illustrated from \[Name\]'s photo and checked by a person before print."
- raisedready.co.uk and the full-colour logo.
- No price, no barcode unless the printer requires one, no seal.

The current cover templates on the options board are drawn at 200 mm; rebuild them at 210 mm once the printer and binding are confirmed.

## "Words for the real moment" page

Every book closes with a spread for the adult: the words to use when the real step happens. It is the visible proof of the "It's your script" promise.

**Structure (same order in every title, about 80 words in total)**

| Block | What it holds | Example (nursery title) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You could say | One or two sentences the adult can say word for word | "We can feel excited and unsure at the same time. I will come back after nursery." |
| Try together | One question to ask the child | "Who could you ask if you needed help?" |
| In real life | One small action for the real day | Choose a simple goodbye phrase to practise together. |
| Read it again | Fixed line, every title | Read this story a few nights in a row, and once more just before the day itself. |

**Rules**

- No outcome claims ("this will help them settle"), no timelines, no deficit words about the child.
- Words are said by the adult to the child, in the story's own vocabulary.
- Event titles (nursery, potty, big bed, new home): the action points to the real day. Adventure titles: one light question only ("What would you have packed for Pip?").
- Each page is approved by Taoufik with the story; titles touching worries or honesty also get a review by someone with early-years experience before launch.

## Voice, words and claims

Raised Ready sounds like an experienced nursery key worker who is also good fun: calm, specific, on your side, never preachy.

| We are | We are not | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | Sugary | One kind line, then the useful part |
| Calm | Preachy | We never tell the parent what they should have done |
| Specific | Vague | "Coat on the peg, one hug, wave at the window" beats "tackle separation anxiety" |
| Honest | Hype | "Helps", "gives you the words"; never "fixes" or "in 3 days" |
| Playful with the child | Silly with the parent | Story text can be funny; parent copy stays grown-up |

**Two registers.** *Story voice* (inside the book): short sentences, sound and rhythm, the child always acting. *Parent voice* (store, ads, emails): plain, warm, practical, second person.

**Words to use and avoid**

| Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| helps, supports, gives you the words, practise, rehearse | fixes, cures, stops, trains, solves | No outcome claims |
| the next step, a big step, when they're ready | in 3 days, dry by September, before school starts (as a promise) | No timelines |
| read it again, night after night | one read and done | Repetition is the evidence-backed dose |
| big feelings, a worry, a wobble | meltdown, naughty, behind, delayed, struggling | No deficit framing; also how a gift buyer embarrasses a parent |
| the adult who reads, you, grown-up | mum (as default) | The reader is often a dad or grandparent |
| illustrated from your photo, it's really them | hand-painted by our artists | The art is AI-made |
| the world they're growing into, things a screen can't teach | AI takeover, future-proof your child | Fear framing |
| brave, curious, kind, honest | genius, smartest, gifted | Praise the skill, not a label |

**Claim rules (binding, from the never-say list):** no toilet-training dates or timelines; never say or imply a child is behind or has a problem; never claim a book fixes, cures or stops a behaviour; never assume mum; never issue or interpret a religious ruling.

**AI honesty.** Ads say "illustrated from your photo". The product page and FAQ say plainly that the illustrations are made with AI and checked by a person before every print.

**Tagline.** No tagline sits under the logo yet. The GB ads test "Ready for the next big step." against "First in the story. Then for real."; the winner goes under the logo.

## Trust, data and legal guardrails

Trust is the product: a parent hands us a photo of their child, so these rules sit above any design or growth choice. This is a working checklist, not legal advice; counsel confirms before launch.

**Children's photos**

- Only a parent or guardian uploads the photo and gives consent, including on gift orders.
- The photo is used only to make that child's book.
- Photos are never used to train models, and the image tool's terms must say so before upload.
- Photos and generated files are deleted after printing, on a set schedule. TJ Books keeps print files 30 days for reprints; agree our own number.
- Signed data processing agreements with the printer and the image-generation provider; a data protection impact assessment before launch.

**Consumer law**

- Personalised books can't be cancelled once ordered; say so before payment.
- Faulty or wrong books are always put right.
- Gift orders: full refund before the photo is added, within 14 days of purchase; the 30-day gift-card conversion and the 12-month expiry are shown at checkout.
- No invented reviews, ratings or customer counts: fake reviews are illegal in the UK.

**Claims.** Every ad, page and book follows the claim rules in the voice section. "Checked by a person before print" stays only while every book really gets that check.

**Trade mark.** File the word mark RAISED READY at the UK IPO before covers go to print, at least in class 16 (printed books) and class 41 (publishing); add more classes if digital editions follow. A clearance search comes first.

**Tax.** A non-UK company selling goods held in the UK usually needs UK VAT registration from the first sale, even though printed books are zero-rated; the accountant confirms.
